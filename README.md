# rocket-league-analytics-innovation
Exploring analytics driven team building and strategy in Rocket League

## Project Overview

  This project is designed to explore the use of analytics to help build better Rocket League teams and/or create more effective strategies. Rather than identify the highest ranked or most skilled players (by statistics),   the project will review how different players, roles, and styles impact individual and teams’ performance. 

  The overall goal is to determine if analytics can identify combinations of players that perform better together or strategies that may create an advantage against specific players.

## Decision-Making Problem

  Rocket League teams must make difficult decisions regarding which players should be placed together, what roles those players have, and what strategies should be used against specific teams. Traditional statistics and     player rankings can show which players perform well individually, but they may not show how those players perform when paired with different teammates or matched against different opponents.

## Proposed Analytics Approach

  This project would utilize both traditional player statistics and more advanced data available from Rocket League replays. Traditional statistics would include goals, assists, shots, saves, wins, and player rankings.      Replay data could provide richer information such as player positioning, movement, boost usage, relative distance from teammates and the ball, and a players time spent in different areas of the field.

  Teams could use this data to compare individual players within a team, teams against other teams, or players against players. This takes the focus away from which players have the best statistics, instead the analyst      would look for relationships and advantages. This would ultimately help to create more competitive teams when the current emphasis is on mechanical/technical ability.

## Use by Decision Makers

  A scout or an owner building a new team would utilize the analysis information first. These decision makers would use information as a starting point for identifying prospective players. Ultimately, they would have        deciding opinion if the analytics solely qualifies a player or not. After this a coach or team manager would utilize this same information for reinforcing desirable player traits, and forming strategies for anticipated    opponents. This would most likely be a cyclical system as the game constantly changes, but would ultimately be another tool create a strategic advantage.

## Connection to Chapter 7

  This project currently connects to chapter 7 through the creative phase of innovation described. At this point the project is taking available data and attempting to create new information that could be used to provide    a competitive advantage. Instead of using existing statistics, the goal is to find new relationships between players, teams, and opponents that could provide decision makers with additional information.

  The project has not reached the prototyping phase. There is currently no finished model or tool for decision makers to use. However, the goal is to develop the idea into something that scouts, coaches, and managers        could use within their existing decision-making processes.

## Prototype Enhancement

  The prototype would continue the ideas of the project by creating player profiles based on relationships identified within the data. Instead of providing decision makers with a collection statistics players would be       categorized by their style, strengths, weaknesses, and compatibility with different players. 

  Using this could improve decision making by leveraging analytical results that are easier for scouts, coaches and managers to use. The scouts could compare potential players based on how they may fit within a team,        while coaches could use the same information to determine player roles and develop strategies against an opponent.

## Prototype Evaluation

  The player profile prototype should be integrated into the main project because it builds on the ability to identify relationships between players. However, the value of the profile would depend on whether the new         information provides decision makers with something they could not easily determine using traditional statistics or their own observations.

  Feedback from scouts, coaches, and managers would help determine which information should be included in the profiles and how it should be presented. Of upmost interest would be whether the identified player styles,       strengths, weaknesses, and compatibility provide useful information when selecting players or developing strategies. Feedback could then be used to refine the entire system.
